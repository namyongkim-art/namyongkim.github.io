KIM NAMYONG — DIFFERENCE
WORKS
DIFFERENCE 2629
82 × 50 cm · Oil on wood panel · 2026
A real wood surface becomes the physical ground on which an imagined space is constructed. Through perspective, light, and shadow, the painted image creates an illusion of depth. What appears to be a solid spatial structure, however, remains a painting on a flat wooden surface.

The work brings together two different conditions within a single plane: the physical presence of real wood and the visual illusion created by painting. Between them emerges a difference between what is actually present and what is perceived.
DIFFERENCE 2664
48 × 80 cm · Oil on wood panel · 2026
The painting constructs an apparent space on the surface of real wood. Perspective, light, and shadow create a sense of depth, while the physical surface remains flat.

The viewer encounters two realities at once: the actual material presence of the wooden panel and the imagined space produced by the painted image. Rather than simply representing space, the work examines how depth is perceived and how a flat image can be experienced as a three-dimensional reality.
DIFFERENCE 2665
48 × 80 cm · Oil on wood panel · 2026
In this work, the flat surface is not merely a support for an image. Through painting, it becomes a site where different levels of depth are visually constructed.

Light, shadow, and perspective suggest a space that appears tangible, yet remains entirely within the plane of the painting. The physical wood and the painted illusion therefore occupy the same surface while functioning in different ways.
DIFFERENCE 2650
43 × 80 cm · Oil on wood panel · 2026
The actual surface of wood and the painted construction of space coexist within a single plane.

Through perspective, light, and shadow, the painting produces an illusion of depth that appears spatially convincing. Yet the viewer remains aware of the physical reality of the wooden surface beneath the image.

This simultaneous experience of material and illusion creates a gap between what is seen and what exists.
DIFFERENCE 2653
34 × 76 cm · Oil on wood panel · 2026
A flat wooden surface becomes a space of visual transformation. Through carefully constructed perspective, light, and shadow, the painting suggests a depth that does not physically exist.

The viewer is drawn into the illusion of space while simultaneously confronted with the physical reality of the surface. This tension between material presence and visual perception is a recurring element in the DIFFERENCE series.
DIFFERENCE 2618
60 × 39 cm · Oil on wood panel · 2026
The work begins with a simple physical fact: the painting is made directly on real wood.

On this material surface, an imagined spatial structure is constructed through painting. Perspective and the contrast of light and shadow generate a sense of depth, although the surface itself remains flat.

The viewer therefore encounters an ambiguity between material and image, between the space that appears and the surface that actually exists.

Working directly on a wooden surface, the artist uses painting to construct an apparent space through perspective, light, and shadow. The physical character of the wood and the illusion created by the painted image coexist within the same plane.

ABOUT

KIM NAMYONG explores the difference between what is seen and what actually exists.

Working directly on real wood panels, he uses oil painting, perspective, light, and shadow to construct spaces that appear to possess physical depth. The actual material of the wood and the painted illusion coexist on a single plane, creating a tension between material presence and visual representation.

His paintings examine how perception transforms a flat surface into an apparent space, and how an image can be experienced as something real even when we know it is an illusion.

From his earlier exploration of Actuality and Reality (實際 / 實在) to the ongoing DIFFERENCE series, his practice focuses on the gap between seeing, perceiving, and recognizing.

Rather than resolving this gap, KIM NAMYONG uses it as a space of inquiry—where the boundary between the actual and the imagined, the flat and the spatial, remains open.
